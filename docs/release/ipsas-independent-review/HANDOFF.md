## 3. Authorization ordering contract (finding 1)
- **Admission:** acquisition of the locks below by `_ipsas_authorize_mutation`, then a fresh re-judgement of authority on a READ COMMITTED snapshot, after the mutation's request and company-period locks and **before** any replay lookup or write, in all five RPCs.
- **Guarantee:** from admission until the mutation's transaction commits, no withdrawal of an authority input completes. A withdrawal that committed before admission is seen by the re-judgement (`forbidden`, `feature_disabled`, `framework_not_ipsas_accrual`). A mutation therefore never records a write under authority withdrawn before its commit.
- **How:** member-capability advisory lock (shared); plan, named-user seats and framework locks (shared); rollout keys **try-locked and never waited for** (open and close take them in opposite orders; unavailable means `authority_changing`); `FOR SHARE` row locks on the person's membership row, open capability rows and the company row (writers' UPDATE/DELETE wait); `lock_timeout` 5 s as an operational protection (not what orders anything).
- **Framework change:** `set_company_reporting_framework` refuses `FRAMEWORK_LOCKED` once a certified trial balance or report exists, so at the mutation level it cannot race a certified company; it is proven at the helper level on an uncertified company.
- **Not claimed:** a clock-based expiry is not a writer; a future writer that takes none of these keys and updates none of these rows is not covered; the readers (`ipsas_*_context`) refuse as soon as a withdrawal commits.

## 4. Download issuance: ordering, owner identity, freshness and response-delivery contract
`precheck (ordinary read, orders nothing) -> sign (Storage, outside any transaction; the link's own exp is read) -> DECIDE (one database transaction) -> freshness again on the host clock -> return the link only if the decision committed as issued and the margin still holds`.

**The decision** (`decide_trial_balance_source_download_issuance`, service role only): in one READ COMMITTED transaction it (a) takes the locks of every writer of the authority inputs, (b) re-reads the owner once the company row is locked and refuses if it changed, (c) re-judges `workspace_authority_basis(user, workspace, 'manage_source_files')` on a fresh statement snapshot, (d) judges freshness last, immediately before the insert, (e) inserts the decision row. **The commit of that transaction is the ordering point**; nothing else is called one. A lock not had in 5 s, or an owner change, returns `unavailable` (nothing recorded, no link, safe to retry).

**Writers found by reading `workspace_authority_basis` and every function/trigger that changes what it reads, and how each is ordered:**
| Authority input | Writers | Ordering with the decision |
|---|---|---|
| open explicit grant | `revoke_workspace_capability`; `firm_members_revoke_workspace_grants` (member removal / re-point) | decision holds `FOR SHARE` on the open grant row to commit: the UPDATE waits (a revoker already holding the row makes the decision wait, re-read, and see the revocation) |
| current plan | licence and entitlement-override writers (trigger takes `cfoclose.plan:<account>` exclusively) | plan advisory lock, shared in the decision |
| named-user seats / billing suspension | seat and suspension writers (`cfoclose.named_user_seats:<account>`) | seats advisory lock, shared, after the plan key (the writers' order) |
| workspace owner / row | the **ownership-change writer: a plain `UPDATE companies SET user_id`** guarded by `trg_companies_entity_capacity`, which takes only the target account's `cfoclose.entity_capacity` lock | `FOR SHARE` on the company row: the UPDATE waits for an admitted decision. **Owner identity:** the plan and seat locks are for the owner read first; after the company row is locked the owner is read again and, if it differs, the decision refuses. It never takes the new owner's locks (an opportunistic second set after other locks are held is how inversions begin); a retry starts from the new owner |
Lock order: plan (S) -> seats (S) -> grant row (S) -> company row (S) -> re-read owner. The entity-capacity key is never taken by the decision, so the transfer writer cannot form a cycle with it. `revoke_workspace_capability` takes the company row (S) then updates the grant row; shared locks do not conflict. The membership row is not read by the predicate and is not locked. A never-member collaborator needs no membership row and is unaffected by anyone else's removal (proven).

**Ordering contract (proven with barriers, never sleeps):**
- *Withdrawal commits first* (before the decision, or holding its locks first): `withdrawn_before_issue`; no `issued` row; response 403; the minted link is discarded unseen and never logged.
- *The decision commits first*: every writer (explicit revoke, member removal, plan lapse, ownership transfer) waits for it, so the withdrawal is ordered after the issuance; the link may be delivered afterwards (a bearer credential until its own expiry).
- *Ownership transferred between the owner read and the company lock*: refused (`decision_unavailable`), nothing recorded; the retry takes the new owner's locks and succeeds; the new owner's later plan withdrawal withdraws access; the old owner's plan stops mattering.

**Expiry and freshness contract.**
- *The signer's own exp, or nothing.* The link's `exp` is read from the token Storage signed. If it cannot be read the link is refused (`signing_failed`, nothing recorded). **There is no estimated-expiry fallback.** Reason, reproduced: Storage truncates the signing instant to a whole second, so the real exp can be up to a second earlier than "signing start + lifetime"; `c615729`'s estimated floor then claimed validity 0.9 s beyond the true exp (the proof prints `a record claims validity until ...15.900Z, after the true exp ...15.000Z`). Timestamp rounding, clock resolution and clock differences were not bounded, and no tolerance was added to make a bound pass.
- *A margin of 5 s* (`DOWNLOAD_MIN_REMAINING_SECONDS`): a **policy** allowance for response transit and modest clock differences between Storage and the database. It is not derived from a measured skew and no skew bound is claimed (clock-skew policy is listed as undecided in 6).
- *Freshness is judged three times:* on the host before any lock is taken (a slow signature); in the database as late as practical, after the locks and immediately before the insert, with the instant and the remaining life recorded; and on the host after the commit, before the response is built. Boundary: exactly the margin is accepted, one millisecond less is refused (unit and constraint tests). A link with less than the margin is never answered `ready`, so `ready` with `expires_in: 0` cannot occur.
- *What no check proves.* A timestamp check does not prove validity at commit or at use: a transaction can pause after its last check (reproduced: the proof pauses the decision after its freshness evaluation until the margin has run out). The decision is then committed as `issued`, the response is withheld (`link_expired`), and the row is left as committed: **a decision can exist without a delivered response**, and immutable history is not rewritten to imply otherwise.

**Separate, documented limitations:** a link ALREADY returned stays valid until its Storage expiry (Storage cannot revoke one signed URL; application withdrawal and the remaining life of an issued bearer link are different things); a clock-based plan expiry is not a writer; a new writer that touches none of the keys above is not covered; the function host's clock and the database clock can differ (signing times are recorded as the host's readings; freshness compares the database clock with the signer's exp); Storage's signing behaviour is exercised only by the real-stack CI job.

## 6. Audit semantics, what is stored, and what is still undecided
- **Four facts, never promoted to one another.** (1) The **authorization/issuance decision** (`outcome`, `authority_evaluated_at`). (2) The **freshness evaluation** (`freshness_evaluated_at`, `freshness_remaining_ms`): the database clock immediately before the insert and the life the signer's exp left then; it proves the link had that much life THEN, not at commit. (3) **Transaction completion**: not recorded (unknowable inside the transaction; may be later than (2)). (4) **Response delivery**: not recorded and not observable by the function. A decision may be recorded without a delivered response; no later fact updates a row. The post-commit host freshness check can withhold a response whose decision is already committed; that is visible only in the operational log (`link_expired`), not as a rewrite of the audit row.
- **Outcomes.** `issued`: authority held under the locks and at least the margin left at the freshness evaluation. `withdrawn_before_issue`: authority gone (freshness not evaluated, columns NULL). `expired_before_issue`: less than the margin left. `denied`: the precheck refused an authenticated caller who named a real upload of a real company. Unauthenticated calls and requests naming an unknown upload or another company's are not recorded. Table constraints pin the outcome to the margin (an `issued` row below the margin, or an `expired` row with the margin left, cannot exist).
- **What is stored.** Actor, company, upload, outcome, signing start/completion (function-host clock), requested lifetime, the signer's exp, the margin, the two evaluation instants, the remaining life at the freshness evaluation, the recorded fingerprint, the database clock at the insert. **Never a URL, token or path.**
- **Implemented reader restrictions (not an accepted policy).** The workspace owner may read the workspace's rows (RLS); the service role has SELECT only; no other role can; append-only (update/delete/truncate refused). The only writers are `decide_...` and `record_..._denial` (service role execute only).
- **Classification of what remains for the OWNER to decide** (none is assumed or accepted on the owner's behalf): (a) who besides the workspace owner may read the records - administrators, auditors, the actor; (b) retention period and deletion rules, and whether denials share them; (c) whether other-company or unknown-upload attempts should be recorded; (d) the clock-skew policy between Storage, the function host and the database, and whether the 5 s margin is the right allowance; (e) whether delivery evidence is wanted at all (it would need Storage access logs, not available here). Until (a)-(e) are decided the audit requirement is NOT claimed complete.
